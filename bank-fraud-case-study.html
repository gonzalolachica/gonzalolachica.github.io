01

Why this exists

This one started after touring Truist's Cyber Fusion Center in Atlanta and reading how much peer-to-peer payment fraud — Zelle scams especially — has become one of the biggest live concerns in banking through 2025–2026. I wanted to see what the actual numbers looked like, and specifically how Truist compared to the other side of that Zelle exposure.

Truist and JPMorgan Chase both co-own Early Warning Services, the company that operates Zelle, which puts both banks directly on the hook for P2P fraud. American Express isn't a Zelle bank at all, but it faces the same fraud/dispute-handling pressure on its own payment products — a useful point of contrast rather than just another data point. Instead of a generic complaint-volume dashboard, I narrowed in specifically on fraud- and scam-related complaints in the public CFPB Consumer Complaint Database.

Data limitation, called out up front: CFPB stopped publishing free-text complaint narratives and its own visualization tool in August 2026. The structured fields — company, product, issue, sub-issue, state, dates — remain fully available via API, so this project is structured-data-only by design, not by omission. That constraint is exactly why the fraud-detection logic in block 04 had to get more careful than a keyword search.

Dataset & architecture

176,927 complaints · Truist, JPMorgan Chase, American Express · 2020–2026

A small end-to-end pipeline built on Microsoft Fabric: a Python notebook pulls CFPB's Complaint Search API one calendar year at a time, a Fabric Data Warehouse (T-SQL) cleans and models the result into a star schema, and Power BI sits on top with Direct Lake on SQL and a 13-measure DAX layer across three report pages.

  • Ingestion — a Fabric Notebook (Python: requests + pandas), pulling year-by-year to stay under the API's result-size cap
  • Warehouse — T-SQL staging tables, then a star schema: fact_complaints + dim_company / dim_date / dim_product / dim_issue / dim_geography
  • Semantic layer — Direct Lake on SQL, 13 DAX measures
  • Report — 3 Power BI pages: Overview, Fraud Profile, Trends

Ingesting a live, 100K+ row API

notebooks/ingest_cfpb_complaints.ipynb

Chase alone has over 100K complaints in scope — enough to hit CFPB's API result-size limit when requested in a single call. The notebook pulls year by year instead (2020–2026), with a browser-style User-Agent header, since CFPB's default request identity gets a 403 from its anti-bot protection. consumer_complaint_narrative is dropped before anything reaches the lakehouse — out of scope by design (see block 01), and also the single riskiest field for parsing corruption during early ingestion attempts.

04

Flagging fraud — the hard part

sql/08_build_dim_issue.sql

Fraud complaints don't live in one clean CFPB category. The direct "Money transfer, virtual currency, or money service" product only covers about 46.5% of them — the rest are scattered across checking/savings accounts, credit cards, and elsewhere.

A first pass at solving this with keyword matching (LIKE '%fraud%', %scam%, %identity theft%) looked reasonable and was wrong: it caught billing complaints about a "credit monitoring / identity theft protection" subscription service — nothing to do with actual identity theft — purely because the category label contained the phrase.

is_fraud_complaint flag logicT-SQL
-- fraud flag = direct P2P/money-transfer product, OR an explicit,
-- manually-reviewed allow-list of issue/sub-issue pairs (11 of 275)
CASE
    WHEN product = 'Money transfer, virtual currency, or money service'
        THEN 1
    WHEN (issue, sub_issue) IN (-- allow-listed pairs, reviewed by hand
        ('Fraud or scam', NULL),
        ('Managing an account', 'Unauthorized transactions or other transaction problem')
        -- ...9 more pairs, see dim_issue
    ) THEN 1
    ELSE 0
END AS is_fraud_complaint
Why this matters: roughly 29% of all fraud complaints get filed under "Checking or Savings Account" rather than the direct money-transfer category — exactly why the filter had to reach beyond Product = "Money transfer..." alone. The allow-list replaced keyword matching entirely once the false positives above turned up.
05

Do Truist, Chase, and Amex actually look alike?

sql/17_analysis_company_category_crosstab.sql

Truist and Chase share identical Zelle/EWS ownership, so the easy assumption is that their fraud profiles look the same. Breaking fraud down by product category per company, with a partitioned window function so each company's percentages total to 100% independently, says otherwise:

17_analysis_company_category_crosstab.sqlT-SQL
SELECT c.company_name, p.product_group, COUNT(*) AS fraud_complaints,
    CAST(COUNT(*) AS DECIMAL(10,4))
        / SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER (PARTITION BY c.company_name) * 100 AS pct_of_company_fraud
FROM fact_complaints f
JOIN dim_company c ON f.company_key = c.company_key
JOIN dim_product p ON f.product_key = p.product_key
WHERE f.is_fraud_complaint = 1
GROUP BY c.company_name, p.product_group
ORDER BY c.company_name, fraud_complaints DESC;
Chase — Money Transfer  51.4%
Truist — Checking/Savings  43.4%
Truist — Money Transfer  40.8%
Amex — Credit Card  46.4%

Chase is money-transfer-dominant, the most P2P-fraud-heavy of the three. Amex's fraud centers on its own core product since it isn't a Zelle bank at all. Truist — despite sharing Chase's exact Zelle/EWS exposure — splits almost evenly between checking/savings and money transfer instead of mirroring Chase.

What the data showed

4.5%→14.8%

Chase leads on both volume and share

14.8% of Chase's total complaints are fraud-related — more than 3× American Express's 4.5%.

9.4%

Smaller bank, not a lower fraud rate

Truist is the smallest of the three by volume but posts the second-highest fraud share at 9.4%.

+19.7%vs-6.8%

Truist trending up fastest, Chase cooling

2026 YoY (annualized for the partial year): Truist +19.7%, Chase -6.8%, Amex essentially flat at +0.6%. This measures complaint counts, not fraud incidence — rising Zelle adoption and awareness can inflate volume on its own.

6.8%vs9.7%

Seasonality is fairly flat

January is a slight peak (9.7% of all fraud complaints, 2020–2025), February the clear low (6.8%). No tax-season or holiday spike showed up.

~50%

Geography tracks population

California, New York, Florida, and Texas together account for roughly half of all fraud complaints — unsurprising, but worth confirming rather than assuming.

11/275

A small, defensible allow-list beat keyword matching

11 of 275 distinct issue/sub-issue pairs are hand-reviewed and flagged as fraud — replacing a keyword search that swept in unrelated billing complaints.
